Note

Why 88% / 110%? These bands aren't a market statistic — they're a negotiation heuristic.
  • The 88% floor approximates a walk-away sanity check: a number meaningfully below target that should prompt you to pause and reassess, not an offer to reject automatically.
  • The 110% stretch is a deliberately ambitious opening ask — high enough to leave room to move down to your actual target without looking unreasonable, a standard anchoring tactic in negotiation.
Treat both as reference points to reason from, not thresholds the tool is telling you to enforce.
Accountability: This tool does not verify that a source, URL, or dollar figure you enter is accurate. That responsibility is yours. Double-check anything you plan to cite in an actual negotiation.

What these inputs mean

Experience (years): years of direct, differentiating experience above what this role already expects — not your total years worked.

Experience premium rate: how much each qualifying year adds to your baseline, before the cap is applied. Default 1.5%/year.

Experience premium cap: the maximum total premium your experience can add, no matter how many years you claim. Default 7.5%.

Geographic differential: adjusts your baseline for cost-of-labor differences in your target city. Default 5% — use BEA Regional Price Parities or BLS OEWS wage data, not a local CPI index.

The experience premium is a teaching assumption, not a labor-market rule. Apply it only to direct, differentiating experience above what the role already expects.

What this is: three reference points derived from one calculation, not three independent numbers. How it's derived: Target = Baseline × (1 + capped experience premium) × (1 + geographic differential); Floor = Target × 0.88; Stretch = Target × 1.10. Why it matters: Target is your primary ask, grounded in the baseline and adjustments above. Floor is a walk-away sanity check, not a number to volunteer. Stretch is your opening ask, leaving room to negotiate down to Target without looking unreasonable. How to use it: open near Stretch, defend Target with the reasoning above, and treat Floor as a private signal to pause and reassess — never state it out loud to a recruiter.

Step 4 — Practice With AI

No API key is used by this tool. Try your response cold first, then get AI feedback — that order matters: attempting it yourself before seeing coaching is what makes the improvement after feedback visible and real.

A. Practice your response

Type what you'd say, or use the microphone to dictate it (Chrome desktop works best — not all browsers support voice input). Try this on your own first — before any AI coaching — so you have something honest to compare against once you get feedback below. If you'd rather have direction or assistance first, skip ahead to B and C, then come back here.

B. Copy this prompt

Paste this into Claude, ChatGPT, or any AI assistant you already have — then come back here once the conversation's done.

Do this — it's where the scoring actually gets its signal. Paste your full conversation from Claude/ChatGPT here. Without it, the scorer in step D only has your own logged lines to work with, which is a much thinner basis for a score.
